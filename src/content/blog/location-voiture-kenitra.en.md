Renting a car in Kenitra should not take half a day of research. And yet, between
advertised rates, the deposit, the driving licence question and local driving
rules, plenty of rentals are decided on gut feeling rather than on information.

This guide brings together what you need to know **before** booking, written by an
agency that rents cars in Kenitra every day.

## Real rates in Kenitra

At Bahnaik My Car, rental starts at **250 MAD per day** for a Dacia Sandero or a
Dacia Logan, and **280 MAD per day** for the Renault Clio 5, Peugeot 208, Opel
Corsa and Hyundai i20.

Three honest caveats:

- These are **base daily rates**, negotiated according to duration. The longer
  the rental, the cheaper the day gets.
- They are **self-drive** rates. That is the most requested option and the only
  one we offer.
- They exclude additional insurance, fuel and any additional driver. All of that
  is discussed at booking time.

If you find it cheaper elsewhere, that is not automatically a bargain: ask what
the price actually includes. A 150 MAD per day rental paired with a very high
deposit is not a 150 MAD rental.

## The deposit: what to know

A deposit is always required for a rental. The amount depends on the vehicle and
on the terms agreed with the agency, and it is released when the car is returned
if everything is in order.

Three practical points:

1. **Ask for the amount in writing** on WhatsApp before coming in. Any agency
   should be able to give you that number without hesitation.
2. **Plan how you will pay the deposit.** Depending on the vehicle, a card
   imprint or a cash deposit may be required.
3. **Do a condition report at pickup and at return**, with photos. It is the
   best protection available, for you and for the agency alike.

We do not publish a single fixed figure on this site: it depends on the car and
the agreed terms, and inventing one here would help nobody. Message us and you
will get a precise answer.

Delivery and collection are **free right across the country**: anywhere inside

In practice, that means that if you land in Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Agadir

## The driving licence: the question that removes half the worry

To rent a car in Morocco on a European licence:

- **A valid licence**, in date, covering the vehicle category. A photo on a
  phone or an expired licence is not enough.
- **An ID card or passport**, in date.
- **A deposit contribution**, depending on the payment method chosen.

International permits are accepted, but a valid national licence is the simplest
to process. If in doubt, send a photo of your licence on WhatsApp before
travelling and you will know straight away whether it is a problem.

## Driving in Morocco: five rules that catch people out

These are the points visitors underestimate most.

- **Seatbelts are compulsory** for every occupant, without exception.
- **Drink driving has a zero tolerance.** The fine is immediate and the
  consequences are heavy, even for an apparently minor breach.
- **Handphones while driving are prohibited**, including hands-free.
- **Leave a margin in town.** Traffic in Kenitra gets dense at peak hours, and
  indicators are not always used by everyone.
- **Weekends and public holidays** can mean noticeably heavier traffic on the
  main roads. Allow extra time.

If you are staying in the city and the surroundings, a small hatchback does the
job perfectly. The Renault Clio 5 is the most requested car in our fleet: compact,

lot of luggage, pick a model with a larger boot, such as the Dacia Logan or the
