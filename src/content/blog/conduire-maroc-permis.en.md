If you are planning to rent a car in Morocco, the first practical question is
simple: **are you allowed to drive, and with what?** This guide covers what
visitors generally need, what you should carry, and the rules that catch people
out. It is practical information, not legal advice.

## The short answer

Most visitors can drive in Morocco with a valid foreign driving licence for the
duration of a short stay. The licence must be:

- **Valid** in its country of issue, with no expired or suspended status.
- **In Roman script (Latin alphabet).** A licence written only in Arabic or
  Cyrillic causes real problems at police checks and with insurance.
- **Held long enough** that you are not a complete novice on the road.

Some nationalities are covered by specific agreements. If you are not sure
whether yours qualifies, ask us before you book, and we will tell you honestly
rather than find out at the counter.

## What to bring with you

Take all of the following. Missing one of them can stop you at a checkpoint.

- **Your original driving licence.** Not a photo, not a copy on your phone.
- **Your passport.**
- **The rental agreement**, with the vehicle details.
- **Your insurance documents**, which the agency will give you.

A digital copy is useful as a backup, but it is not a substitute. Police may ask
for the physical document, and an agency cannot invent one for you.

## If your licence is not in Latin script

This is the single most common problem, and it is worth sorting before you fly.

If your licence uses a script you cannot read in Latin letters, some drivers
carry an **official translation** or an **international driving permit**. Ask your
issuing authority what is available in your country, because the rules differ.

We cannot advise you on foreign law, and we will not issue documents. What we
will do is tell you, before you book, whether our experience suggests a problem,
so you are not surprised.

## The equipment that matters

This is the part people forget, and it is the part that matters most in Morocco.

**Every day, in every season:**

- **Seat belts** for every passenger. Not optional, and not negotiable with the
  agency.
- **A working phone**, charged, with the agency number saved.
- **Water** in the car. Moroccan summers are hot, and you may be stuck in traffic.

**Do not drive without:**

- A **first-aid kit** in the car.
- A **warning triangle** ("triangle de signalisation" in French).

## Driving rules that catch people out

These are the ones we see most often with visitors.

**Speed.** Limits are posted on signs and are enforced. Police use radar. Do not
assume a wide, empty road means you can go faster.

**Seat belts.** Worn by everyone, in the front and the back.

**Mobile phones.** Hand-held driving is prohibited. Use a hands-free kit if you
need the phone, and better, pull over.

**Alcohol.** Driving after drinking is illegal and it is treated seriously. The
legal limit is low, and enforcement is real.

**Overtaking.** Drive on the right, overtake on the left, and expect others not
to.

## Moroccan driving habits you will notice

You will see things that differ from Europe, and knowing them in advance makes
the trip calmer.

- **Lane discipline is looser.** Motorcycles may filter between lanes. Allow for
  it.
- **Signals are used less consistently.** Do not assume a missing indicator
  means nothing is happening behind you.
- **Roundabouts follow the usual priority rule**, but entry is not always polite.
- **Petrol stations on the autoroute are infrequent.** Refuel before you get
  on, not on it.

None of this is dangerous. It just requires anticipation.

## Insurance and the excess

The rate you pay includes basic insurance and theft cover. It does not mean
everything is covered at zero cost.

- There is normally an **excess** (franchise) that you are responsible for.
- Tyres, glass and the underside are often excluded. Read the terms.
- Ask what the excess is **before** you sign, and photograph the car at
  collection.

If you want to reduce the excess, ask about it when booking. It is cheaper to
arrange than to argue about later.

## If you have an accident

In order of priority:

1. **Check for injuries** and call emergency services if needed.
2. **Do not move the vehicles** until police attend, unless someone is in danger.
3. **Call the agency immediately.** We will guide you through the process.
4. **Photograph the scene** if it is safe to do so.
5. **Do not admit liability** or sign anything you do not understand.

A calm, documented accident is resolved quickly. A rushed one is not.

## A practical checklist

Before you collect the car:

- Licence valid, in Latin script, original with you.
- Passport with you.
- Insurance documents received.
- Excess amount known.
- Phone charged, agency number saved.
- Seat belts working.
- Air conditioning working (it matters, especially in summer).

## In short

- Most visitors can drive on a valid Latin-script licence.
- Always carry the original, not a copy.
- Non-Latin licences need a translation or permit: check before you fly.
- Seat belts are mandatory for everyone.
- Know your excess before you sign.
- In an accident: injuries first, then call us.
