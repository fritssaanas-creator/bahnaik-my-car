The deposit is the thing clients ask us about most, and rightly so: it is the
only part of the file you cannot read at a glance from a brochure. Here is a
complete explanation, without beating about the bush.

## Why a deposit exists

It is not an arbitrary precaution. The deposit covers what insurers call the
"excess": the part of the damage that stays yours if a claim happens during the
rental.

Without a deposit, an agency carries a disproportionate risk: a deep scratch, a
damaged bumper, a stolen part, and there is no way to recover. The deposit is
simply the counterbalance to that risk.

In practice, the deposit covers:

- Accidental damage, up to the excess.
- Insurance excesses, which remain the renter's responsibility.
- Shortfalls: missing fuel, an extra cleaning fee, a forgotten item.

## What makes the amount vary

There is no single grid, and that is normal. The amount depends on:

- **The vehicle.** A small hatchback and a saloon are not worth the same, so they
  do not carry the same risk.
- **The length of the rental.** A short rental generally asks for less than a
  long one on the same car, because the wear is lower.
- **The payment method.** A card imprint and a cash deposit are handled
  differently, for the agency and for you.
- **The options chosen**: additional driver, mileage, extra cover.

## How to prepare for it

Three practical tips, in order:

1. **Ask for the amount before you travel.** One WhatsApp message is enough. You
   should be able to get that number without having to come in first.
2. **Ask which payment methods are accepted.** If the agency requires a card
   imprint and the card you have does not work abroad, better to know in advance.
3. **Ask about the refund procedure.** How it is returned, over what delay, and
   what happens in the case of minor damage.

## The condition report is your protection

This is the most underestimated step, and it protects **both parties**.

At pick-up and at return:

- Do a **complete walkaround** of the car, outside and in.
- **Photograph everything**: bodywork, wheels, windscreen, interior, mileage, fuel
  level.
- **Note every defect** on the rental document, however small.
- Compare the pick-up and return photos.

In a dispute, those photos are the only evidence that counts. A scratch recorded
at pick-up will not be blamed on you at return.

## What a refund actually looks like

The normal course, with no incident:

1. You return the vehicle at the agreed time.
2. The condition report is done, with photos.
3. The deposit is **refunded**, using the same method it was taken with.

With a minor incident, such as a broken mirror:

1. The assessment is done, with photographs.
2. You are told the exact amount being withheld.
3. Only the part corresponding to the damage is withheld, if you took the cover
   that caps what you owe.

## What we do not do

We do not publish an "indicative" figure on this site. An invented number to fill
a page would be either wrong or misleading, and it would give you a bad basis for
negotiation.

We do not promise a "zero deposit" either: that would be false. A deposit is
required on every rental, and we would rather tell you that plainly.

What we do commit to is simpler:

- The amount is communicated **before** you travel.
- The refund conditions are explained **before** you sign.

## How to ask the right question

End your message with: "What is the deposit for this car, over this duration, and
which payment methods do you accept for the refund?"

That is precise, it is professional, and it is exactly what any serious agency
should be able to answer.

## In short

- The deposit covers the excess, not an arbitrary figure.
- The amount depends on the car, the duration, the payment method and the options.
- Ask **before** coming in, along with the payment method.
- Do the condition report **with photos** at pick-up and return.
- No honest agency can display one fixed universal figure.
