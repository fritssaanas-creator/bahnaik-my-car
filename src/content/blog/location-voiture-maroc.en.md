Morocco suits a rental car very well. The roads are in good condition, distances
stay manageable, and a vehicle gives you a freedom no bus or train can offer.
You just need to organise it properly.

Here is the guide we give our customers before a stay.

## Choosing the right car for the length of the stay

The criterion that decides everything: **how many days, and how many kilometres**.

- **1 to 3 days in Kenitra and around**: a hatchback is enough. Renault Clio 5 or
  Opel Corsa. Low consumption, easy to manoeuvre in town, limited cost.
- **A week including Rabat and Casablanca**: a comfortable compact, such as the
  Peugeot 208 or Hyundai i20. Same price bracket, more comfort on long stretches.
- **Two weeks or more, with luggage**: a saloon with a generous boot, such as the
  Dacia Logan. The price difference is small and the trip genuinely improves.

Across our fleet, the gap between the cheapest and the dearest category is a few
dozen dirhams per day. The choice is about luggage volume and duration, not about
a major budget difference.

## How much time to allow for a typical journey

As a rough guide, on a national road in normal conditions:

- **Kenitra to Rabat**: about 45 minutes.
- **Kenitra to Casablanca**: about 1 h 15.
- **Kenitra to Tangier**: about 2 h 30.
- **Kenitra to Chefchaouen**: about 2 h 30.

These figures move a lot with traffic, weather and season. In other words:
**treat them as a floor, never as a promise.** If you have a flight or a train
to catch, add generous margins.

## Driving rules to know before you leave

Morocco is not a country where you drive as in Europe, but it is not a country
without rules either. What matters:

- **Driving on the right**, as in continental Europe.
- **Seatbelts compulsory** for everyone.
- **Alcohol and driving do not mix.** Zero tolerance.
- **Handphones while driving are prohibited.**
- **Stop lights and right-of-way are enforced**, but stay alert: traffic in town
  demands more anticipation than in Europe, especially at roundabouts and lane
  changes.

## Road conditions

The main roads are paved and well maintained. Secondary roads can be more uneven,
with cobblestones or potholes. Nothing unusual if you come from Europe, but slow
down at night: lighting is limited outside built-up areas.

Also plan to **fill up before leaving towns**. Stations are regular along the
main arteries, far less so along secondary roads.

## Budget: what to expect

For a one-week stay with a hatchback, the order of magnitude is:

- **Rental**: from 250 MAD per day, so roughly 1,750 MAD for seven days, before
  adjustment for duration.
- **Fuel**: variable, and the line that fluctuates most from one trip to another.
- **Deposit**: set aside, refunded at the end of the rental.

The rest depends entirely on you. A rental car in Morocco is rarely the largest
expense of a trip, and it is often the one that most changes its quality.
